Poplavok is a restaurant building on the Dnipro River in Dnipro. Its subject is simple: a public building placed on the water, close enough to the embankment to belong to the city and far enough into the river to feel separate.

The building is usually described through its shape. Local history calls it a "flying saucer" on the water. That is a useful description, because the round volume and the horizontal deck are the first things the eye reads.[^city]

<aside><h4>Two dates</h4>The restaurant's own site says it has operated since 1976. A city history article says Poplavok appeared in November 1968. The post keeps the range because both dates are public.</aside>

## Site

The restaurant belongs to the Soviet-period embankment of Dnipro. The city article places it in the same visual story as the nearby Ukrgipromez institute building and the postwar development of the riverfront in the 1950s and 1960s.[^city]

Sergei Brusov is named there as the architect. He was chief architect of Ukrgipromez from 1959 to 1977 and designed both the institute building and Poplavok beside it.[^city]

> A small building became the accent because it was placed where the city was open.

The structure does not need a tall silhouette. It has the river, the deck, the round body and the bridge to the entrance. The approach is part of the object.

## Use

The official restaurant site gives the current public version of the building. It describes Poplavok as placed directly on the water, with views to both banks of the Dnipro. It also notes a 2014 modernization: the first floor became the main restaurant, and the second floor became a hall for events.[^restaurant]

The same page gives capacities after that work: up to 120 guests for private events and up to 300 people for concerts and parties. Those numbers describe the building now better than old slogans do.

<aside><h4>What stayed</h4>The use changed less than the interiors. It remained a public leisure building on the water, reached by the same kind of short ceremonial approach from the embankment.</aside>

## Form

Poplavok works because it does one thing clearly. It turns the river into the room's outer wall. The city is seen across the water, and the building is seen from the city as a round platform on it.

The form belongs to the space-age language of the 1960s. The city source says that theme was active in Dnipro architecture of the period. Poplavok is the compact version of that idea: a saucer, a deck, a river.

The building survived the post-Soviet change of restaurant culture. That is why it still matters as architecture. A form made for a specific public habit kept working after the habit changed.
